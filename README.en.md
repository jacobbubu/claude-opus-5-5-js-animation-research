# Opus 5.5 Browser Code Animations: 30 Examples Ranked by Views

Research snapshot: 2026-09-28, logged-in X session in Chrome. Only posts where the author explicitly attributes the work to **Claude Opus 5.5**, and where the original post, an author reply, or the source code confirms a **JavaScript / HTML / Canvas / SVG / WebGL / Three.js / p5.js / Remotion** animation, are included. View counts are snapshots taken when each post was visited; they do not represent unique viewers, video plays, or quality scores. Model attribution relies on the authors' own statements; only visible text and some public source code were verified, and not every project was independently re-run.

This list was re-filtered and re-ranked by technical criteria. The last few entries have very low view counts and are included in the 30 only because they meet the technical criteria; **they are not called viral**. Posts without evidence of a JS/HTML implementation, or that mainly use Unity, After Effects, or Manim, are excluded.

Videos and screenshots come from each author's public posts; credits and links to the original posts are listed for every entry. Each example can be watched directly in the players below, or the original MP4 can be downloaded from the repository. 8 larger videos use compressed playback versions under 10 MB; the download links keep the originals. Media is used for research and review; the original authors retain all rights to their work.

### 2. Single-file HTML UI morphing motion · @twoclipping

- **Author, date and original post:** [@twoclipping, 2026-09-25](https://x.com/twoclipping/status/2103273003555402193).

![Screenshot of case 2](media/strict/twoclipping-2103272964967804928-preview.jpg)

- **Local video:** [Play or download MP4](media/strict/twoclipping-2103272964967804928.mp4).

**Watch inline:**

https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8b33ecc0-999b-442b-a8f4-b739208036d9
- **Animation and tech:** One shape morphs continuously between a button, loader, player, slider, tab, chart, and command palette. The author says the whole video was made in code, and the published template explicitly requires a single 1440×1440 HTML file.
- **Prompt:** What the author published is a reusable template, not necessarily the verbatim input for this particular film. Excerpt of the original:

> One HTML file, square 1440x1440. Every style is computed from time inside seek(t): no CSS transitions, no timers, no state carried between frames.

[The original post contains the full XML template](https://x.com/twoclipping/status/2103273003555402193), with six sections: inputs, direction, structure, build, gotchas, and start.
- **Process:** The rhythm is 120 BPM over 7 bars; before the full render, one frame per beat is rendered for review. Springs are closed-form step responses; Playwright captures 4 subframes per frame, and ffmpeg `tmix` blends them into 60 fps motion blur. The author warns not to use `will-change` on scaled camera elements, and says the ending must match the opening in frame, cursor position, and velocity.
- **Visible engagement:** 11,794 likes, 956,309 views.

## Appendix A: Full prompt by @twoclipping

Below is the original English prompt from the [original post](https://x.com/twoclipping/status/2103835273813496100), with the original capitalization and punctuation preserved.

    <inputs>
    Ask me for: a one-word brand name for the wordmark (a verb works best), 9 to 12 high-res photos, a royalty-free song around 120 BPM with a drop and a quiet breakdown (Mixkit, free for commercial use), and a free stock clip of a plain wall with moving plant shadows (Pexels).
    </inputs>

    <direction>
    An Apple-keynote launch film, 2D only, one continuous take. Every scene is made out of the previous one: nothing fades, blurs or cuts. Objects change shape instead: text rises out of a mask line, icons pop from zero on a spring, bars draw across, pages push, and a black shape floods the whole frame and contracts into the next scene. Warm off-white canvas, black UI, iOS 26 liquid glass over the photos. Archivo (wdth 125, weight 800) for the wordmark, Geist for UI. A cursor drives every change with real clicks, drags and long-presses. The camera zooms screen-studio style so each moment fills the square, and the cursor scales with it.
    Banned: crossfades, blur-ins, brightness "developing", 3D flips, particles, glows, holds longer than 1s, anything that looks like a template.
    </direction>

    <structure>
    120 BPM, 54 beats, something happens on every beat.
    Open: the wordmark squeezes into its own period like an accordion, the dot grows into a black pill, a label rises inside it. Click: six iris blades close over the label and snap open onto a photo. The circle becomes a square, shrinks, the grid unfolds from behind it like a paper map (center, plus, corners), reflows into a bento, and a click zooms into one tile, landing exactly on the drop.
    Glass: a glass word pops in letter by letter, melts into a droplet that stretches into a glass toolbar. The adjust icon turns it into a slider. Dragging relights the photo from day to golden hour (two aligned shots), and the knob turns into a glass lens while held. It lifts into a glass orb, the next photo opens inside it as a circle, and the orb expands into a lock screen: glass clock digits, date, and a home bar that stretches into a glass music player.
    Stage: the lock screen pulls back into a phone (the bezel grows out of the screen edge). The Dynamic Island stretches like liquid, pinches off, flies over and grows into a Mac window that rolls up like a blind. Long-press the wallpaper, drag it onto a Safari tab, the page pushes in, drop it and it becomes the hero of a landing page. Scroll: the hero morphs into a framed print on a product card, with the mat and molding growing out of the photo's edge. Pick a frame color (it paints across) and a size, then the nav button flies down into "Order print".
    Order: one black shape keeps morphing: Ordered ✓ → Printing % → On its way (a van on a route) → Delivered ✓.
    Wall: the delivered circle floods the frame edge to edge, holds black for a beat, and contracts into the framed print hanging on the real wall footage. The same iris opens onto the print, closes again, the frame floods the screen and contracts into the pill → the dot → the letters spring back out, landing on the beat return. Last frame = first frame.
    </structure>

    <build>
    1. One HTML file, square 1440x1440. Every style is computed from time inside an async seek(t): no CSS transitions, no timers, no state between frames.
    2. Springs are closed-form step responses. A value with many targets is the sum of one spring per change, so it stays a pure function of time.
    3. Liquid glass: each glass element holds its own clone of the scene behind it, filtered with an SVG feImage displacement map (a rounded-rect distance field) through three feDisplacementMaps at slightly different scales for chromatic edges, plus a rim light. Glass letters: a canvas distance field per glyph gives the map, mask and highlights.
    4. Goo: blur + alpha threshold, then composite the source atop it so the glass stays sharp inside.
    5. Iris: 6 blades around a hexagonal aperture. Each blade is its two vertices, both edge extensions and the SHORT arc between them.
    6. Wordmark squeeze: every letter moves toward the dot by the same factor and its drawn width follows (narrow the wdth axis, scale the rest), so the letters stay touching.
    7. Footage: re-encode all-intra (ffmpeg -g 1), load it as a blob URL, await 'seeked' before drawing each frame.
    8. Sound: a downloaded SFX for every event (Mixkit), never synthesized, each placed by its measured peak. The song starts on a downbeat: the zoom lands on the drop, the wall sits in the breakdown, the wordmark returns with the beat. Loudnorm to -14 LUFS.
    9. Render with Playwright: 4 subframes per frame blended with ffmpeg tmix, 60fps. Check one frame per beat, then scan for single-frame pops (frame-difference spikes 3x their neighbours).
    </build>

    <gotchas>
    backdrop-filter: url() misreads displacement maps in Chromium, so clone the scene instead. A flood must overscale past the corners and take about 0.3s, or half the screen changes in one frame. A child with visibility: visible shows through a hidden parent, so use inherit. Text that swaps inside a morphing shape needs its own mask. python http.server can't range-seek video, so use the blob URL.
    </gotchas>

    <start>
    Ask me for the inputs, then show me the beat map and 4 stills (open, glass, stage, wall) before you write the full film.
    </start>

## Common Methods & Tips

- **Single-file browser works are easy to reproduce and share.** [@MengTo](https://x.com/MengTo/status/2103155074705019158) asks for a final standalone page; [@victormustar](https://x.com/victormustar/status/2102707412704919910), [@jurlycat](https://x.com/jurlycat/status/2102645793828036643), and [@Voxyz_ai](https://x.com/Voxyz_ai/status/2102531681450119426) all explicitly use a single HTML file.
- **Make the picture a pure function of time for frame-by-frame rendering.** [@twoclipping's public template](https://x.com/twoclipping/status/2103273003555402193) requires `seek(t)` to determine each frame's state with no state carried across frames; [@dotey's reconstructed prompt](https://github.com/JimLiu/taohuayuan/blob/main/docs/prompt.md) likewise requires state to be restored from the scene number and in-scene progress.
- **Check key frames first, then do the full export.** [@twoclipping](https://x.com/twoclipping/status/2103273003555402193) suggests rendering one frame per beat first, then exporting with Playwright and ffmpeg; [@MengTo](https://x.com/MengTo/status/2103155074705019158) requires screenshots in four lighting states across the day and a console check.
- **Trade low resolution and procedural constraints for a distinctive style.** [@victormustar](https://x.com/victormustar/status/2102707412704919910) limits the horse to 128×96 and 12 poses with IK; [@akiy_8](https://x.com/akiy_8/status/2103434383218872503) chooses pure WebGL2 and math formulas to simulate halftone dots and misregistration.
- **Use actionable acceptance checklists for complex 3D scenes.** [@MengTo](https://x.com/MengTo/status/2103155074705019158) itemizes water flow, weather, boat controls, and clipping checks; [@akakuma0219](https://x.com/akakuma0219/status/2103820736519708709) suggests pointing out 1–2 specific issues per round before adding gameplay and performance optimization.
- **Sound can be generated in code alongside the visuals.** [@jurlycat](https://x.com/jurlycat/status/2102645793828036643) uses Canvas 2D + Web Audio; [@Voxyz_ai](https://x.com/Voxyz_ai/status/2102531681450119426) uses JS visuals and music synthesized in Python; [@techartist_](https://x.com/techartist_/status/2104186357824487573) lists Tone.js, Web Audio, and piano samples.
